# Briefing — Recent AML Enforcement Landscape

**Date:** December 1, 2025
**Document type:** Market/regulatory briefing (Compliance Department)
**Status:** Current contextual briefing for the Board Risk Committee

---

## Purpose

A short briefing on the current AML enforcement environment, prepared for the Board Risk Committee ahead of the examination cycle. This is context, not a finding; it explains why supervisory attention to transaction monitoring and alert backlogs has intensified across the industry.

## The trend

Across U.S. banking supervision over the past several cycles, examiners have sharpened their focus on a recurring set of BSA/AML weaknesses:

- **Transaction-monitoring effectiveness.** Regulators increasingly probe whether monitoring is genuinely risk-based, whether scenario thresholds are justified, and whether monitoring coverage survives system changes — including core-platform migrations, which have repeatedly produced monitoring gaps at peer institutions.
- **Alert and case backlogs.** Backlogs of uninvestigated alerts have featured in multiple supervisory findings and enforcement actions, on the theory that an unreviewed alert is an unreviewed risk of unreported suspicious activity.
- **Governance and accountability.** Findings increasingly cite weak senior accountability and inadequate board-level management information, not just technical control gaps.

## Why it matters for Brightmoor

Brightmoor's risk profile — a commercial-heavy regional bank with cash-intensive business customers, undergoing a core-platform migration — sits squarely in the areas examiners are pressing. Peer institutions that treated monitoring tuning, backlog throughput, and program governance as afterthoughts have drawn MRAs and, in some cases, consent orders carrying remediation obligations and monetary penalties. The cost of a credible, proactive program is materially lower than the cost of a formal enforcement action.

## Takeaway for the Board

The Board should expect transaction monitoring, alert backlogs, and program governance to be focal points of supervisory attention, and should support a program posture that treats them as such. (This briefing predates the January 2026 examination findings; it is included to frame why those findings track the broader enforcement landscape.)
