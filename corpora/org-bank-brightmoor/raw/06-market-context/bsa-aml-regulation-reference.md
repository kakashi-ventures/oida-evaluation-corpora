# Reference — BSA/AML Requirements (Transaction Monitoring, SAR Filing, CDD)

**Date:** October 1, 2025
**Document type:** Standing reference summary (Compliance Department)
**Status:** Current external requirement — durable reference, not a Brightmoor work product

---

## What this is

A plain summary of the principal Bank Secrecy Act / anti-money-laundering obligations the Bank is held to. It is a durable external standard; it does not change with any individual examination or internal project. It is maintained as the reference the program works against.

## The Bank Secrecy Act program

U.S. banks must maintain a written BSA/AML compliance program reasonably designed to assure and monitor compliance with the BSA. The program must include, at minimum, the "pillars":

1. A system of **internal controls**.
2. **Independent testing** of the program.
3. A designated **BSA compliance officer** responsible for day-to-day compliance.
4. **Training** for appropriate personnel.
5. Risk-based **customer due diligence (CDD)**, including identifying and verifying beneficial owners of legal-entity customers and understanding the nature and purpose of customer relationships to develop a customer risk profile.

## Transaction monitoring

Banks must monitor for and detect suspicious activity. Monitoring is expected to be **risk-based** — calibrated to the institution's products, customers, and geographies — rather than applied as a single uniform rule. Monitoring systems generate alerts; alerts must be investigated and dispositioned on a timely basis. A backlog of uninvestigated alerts is a program weakness because potentially suspicious activity may go unreviewed and unreported.

## Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)

A bank must file a **Currency Transaction Report** for cash transactions exceeding **$10,000** in a business day (aggregated). Note that the $10,000 CTR threshold is a *currency-reporting* obligation and is **distinct from monitoring thresholds**: a monitoring threshold set at a round $10,000 may miss "structuring" — deliberate transactions kept just under a reporting threshold — which is itself reportable suspicious activity.

## Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)

A bank must file a **Suspicious Activity Report** when it knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that a transaction involves funds from illegal activity, is designed to evade BSA requirements (including structuring), has no apparent lawful purpose, or otherwise appears suspicious. SARs are filed with FinCEN, generally within **30 days** of initial detection of facts that may constitute a basis for filing. Whether an alert is "SAR-eligible" is determined on investigation; an alert firing is not, by itself, a SAR.

## Supervisory consequences

Regulators examine BSA/AML programs and may communicate deficiencies as supervisory findings (e.g., a Matter Requiring Attention) or, for more serious or unremediated weaknesses, through formal enforcement (e.g., a consent order with monetary penalties). Effective, documented, risk-based programs are the expectation.
