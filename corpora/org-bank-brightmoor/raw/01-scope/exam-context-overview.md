This short note explains, in plain language, what just happened and the vocabulary the remediation team will use. It is orientation only; the authoritative finding is the regulator's MRA letter and the exam findings document.

## What is a BSA/AML examination?

Every U.S. bank is examined by its primary federal regulator for compliance with the Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money-laundering (AML) rules. Examiners review whether the Bank has an effective program to detect and report suspicious activity — transaction monitoring, customer due diligence (CDD), and the filing of Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs). Brightmoor's most recent fieldwork ran in **December 2025**.

## What is an MRA?

A **Matter Requiring Attention (MRA)** is a formal supervisory finding. It is not (yet) an enforcement action or a consent order — it is a written deficiency the regulator expects the Bank to remediate on a defined timeline, with progress reported back. Left unremediated, MRAs can escalate to formal enforcement (for example, a consent order). The practical posture is: this is serious, it is fixable, and the regulator will come back to validate that we fixed it.

## What did our MRA say (in brief)?

Three things, which map to our three workstreams:

1. **Monitoring gaps** — our transaction-monitoring system was not catching everything it should, including issues tied to the core-system migration.
2. **Alert backlog** — too many monitoring alerts had not been investigated within expected timeframes.
3. **Governance** — the program's senior accountability and oversight needed strengthening.

## What happens next?

We respond formally, we run the remediation program (Project Cornerstone), we report progress, and the regulator validates. The goal is to close the MRA at validation. Nothing in this note changes the formal requirements in the MRA letter — read that for the authoritative wording.
