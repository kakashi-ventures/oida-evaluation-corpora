# Meeting 002: Transaction-Monitoring Threshold Debate

**Date:** March 6, 2026
**Location:** Risk Conference Room
**Attendees:** Dana Whitfield (CCO, chair), Priya Nair (Head of Financial Crimes), Lena Cho (Head of Retail Banking), Marcus Hale (CRO), Raj Patel (Internal Audit)
**Purpose:** Debate the monitoring-threshold change ahead of the steering decision

---

[14:00] Whitfield: The regulator's March 2 follow-up was explicit: flat $10,000 is inadequate, they expect risk-based. This session is to surface the trade-offs honestly before steering makes the binding call. Priya, frame the proposal.

[14:03] Nair: Move from a $10,000 fixed threshold to a $5,000 **risk-based** threshold. The $5k is a baseline trigger; the effective floor flexes by customer risk rating and product. Low-risk consumer accounts sit higher; cash-intensive businesses and higher-risk segments sit at or below $5k. More coverage where the risk is, which is the whole point of "risk-based." Yes, it raises alert volume — that's the cost of catching structuring below $10k, which the regulator told us is happening.

[14:09] Cho: And here's the cost. I ran Retail's numbers. Dropping the floor to $5k, even risk-weighted, pulls a large volume of perfectly ordinary consumer transactions into review — small-business deposits, people moving money between their own accounts, cash-heavy but legitimate customers. Each one is potential friction: a hold, an outreach call, sometimes an abandoned transaction or a closed account. I am **not** saying don't comply. I am saying lower thresholds are not free, the cost lands on real customers and on my front line, and we should choose it with eyes open, not pretend the only cost is analyst hours.

[14:15] Nair: That's fair and I don't want to wave it away. The risk-based design is precisely to keep the low-risk consumer floor higher so we're not drowning ordinary customers. But I won't pretend friction goes to zero. Where Lena and I differ is the tiebreaker: when coverage and friction genuinely conflict, I think coverage has to win, and so does the appetite statement.

[14:20] Cho: I don't disagree that coverage wins the tiebreaker. My objection is narrower: I want the friction quantified and managed — better tuning, faster outreach, clear customer comms — as a *condition* of going to $5k, not an afterthought. If we lower the threshold and do nothing about the customer experience, we'll create a different problem.

[14:25] Patel: Audit's view: the regulator has effectively removed the flat-$10k option, so the real decision is how to implement risk-based well, not whether. Lena's friction conditions are reasonable and should be written into the rollout.

[14:29] Hale: I'm hearing consensus on direction — risk-based, $5k baseline — with Lena's friction-management conditions attached. That's what I'll take to steering for the binding decision. Lena, your objections go in the record; they're conditions, not blockers.

[14:32] Whitfield: Agreed. Direction leans clearly to the $5k risk-based model, conditioned on a friction-management plan. Steering decides and issues the policy.
