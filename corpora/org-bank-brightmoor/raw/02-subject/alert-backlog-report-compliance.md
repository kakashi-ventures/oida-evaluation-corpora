# Alert Backlog Report — Compliance / Financial Crimes

**Date:** February 6, 2026
**Document type:** Backlog status report (Financial Crimes / AML)
**Prepared by:** Priya Nair, Head of Financial Crimes
**Status:** Current Compliance figure of record for the alert backlog

---

## Headline

As of the February 6, 2026 reconciliation, the Bank carries a backlog of approximately **1,400 uninvestigated transaction-monitoring alerts**. This is the figure Compliance reports to the program and to the Board Risk Committee, and it is the count we will measure remediation against.

## How this figure is counted

This backlog count is **deliberately inclusive**. It counts as "uninvestigated" every alert that has not been through a documented analyst disposition, **including alerts the Sentinel platform auto-closed** under its own scoring rules without analyst review. We count auto-closed alerts in the backlog on purpose: the MRA criticism is precisely that potentially suspicious activity may not have been reviewed, and an alert the system closed by itself has not been reviewed by a person. For an MRA-grade backlog we do not give ourselves credit for machine auto-closures.

This is why our number is higher than the vendor dashboard's "open alerts" figure (≈900). The Sentinel dashboard counts only alerts currently sitting in an analyst queue and **excludes the auto-closed population**; it therefore understates the remediation population we are accountable for. Both numbers are "real," but they count different things, and for remediation purposes the inclusive ~1,400 is the number that matters. We do not consider the ~900 dashboard figure adequate for board or regulator reporting.

## Note on SAR-eligibility

An earlier preliminary triage memo (February 3) suggested that essentially all ~1,400 alerts were SAR-eligible. **That characterization was overstated and has been corrected/retracted** — see this report's triage tiers above. On preliminary triage the great majority of the backlog is low-risk; SAR-eligibility can only be determined on investigation, and the high-risk priority queue (≈180) is where genuine SAR candidates are expected to concentrate.
