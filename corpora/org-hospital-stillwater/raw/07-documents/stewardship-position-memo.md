# Antimicrobial Stewardship Position Memo — On a 1-Hour Antibiotic Mandate

**Date:** March 6, 2026
**Author:** Elena Soto, MD — Infectious Disease; Chair, Antimicrobial Stewardship Program
**To:** Sepsis Steering Committee
**Re:** Proposed universal 1-hour door-to-antibiotic target

---

## Position

I support faster antibiotics for septic shock. I **do not support** a blanket 1-hour broad-spectrum antibiotic mandate applied to all patients with suspected sepsis, and I want the rationale on the record before the protocol is finalized.

## The evidence does not say what the proposal implies

Dr. Tran's proposal cites the association between earlier antibiotics and lower mortality. That association is **strongest, and arguably only robust, for septic shock**. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign's 2021 "1-hour bundle" recommendation was itself downgraded and remains contested precisely because the data for *undifferentiated, non-shock* sepsis are weak. There is **no high-quality evidence** that a 1-hour mandate reduces mortality in sepsis without shock.

## The harm of a blanket mandate is real and measurable

A universal 1-hour clock, enforced as a quality metric, predictably drives:

- Broad-spectrum antibiotics started on patients who turn out not to be infected (we already see this; our *C. difficile* rate and our carbapenem days-of-therapy are not trivial).
- Pressure to skip blood cultures or source assessment to "beat the clock."
- Anchoring on infection when the differential is still open.

These are not hypothetical; they are the documented downsides of time-to-antibiotic metrics in the stewardship literature. A mortality benefit that does not exist for non-shock sepsis cannot justify them.

## Recommendation

**Stratify.** 1-hour target for septic shock (where the evidence supports urgency); 3-hour target for sepsis without shock, with antimicrobial selection per the stewardship empiric table. This captures the benefit where it is real and avoids the harm where the benefit is not demonstrated.

I will support v2.0 if it stratifies. I will formally dissent if it imposes a universal 1-hour broad-spectrum mandate.
