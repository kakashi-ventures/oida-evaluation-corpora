# Meeting 003: Protocol v2.0 Design Workshop

**Date:** March 12, 2026
**Location:** Boardroom B
**Attendees:** Karen Holloway (facilitator), Marcus Tran (ED), Elena Soto (ID/Stewardship), Janet Mwangi (Nursing), Priyanka Rao (ICU)
**Duration:** 2 hours

---

[13:05] Holloway: Today we resolve antibiotic timing. Marcus wants one hour for everyone. Elena wants stratification. We are not leaving without a decision both of you can sign.

[13:08] Tran: My position hasn't moved. One hour, door to antibiotic, anyone we suspect of sepsis. Simple, fast, measurable, and the mortality literature backs urgency.

[13:10] Soto: The literature backs urgency *in shock*. I circulated the evidence brief — the one-hour bundle was downgraded precisely because the data for non-shock sepsis are weak. A universal one-hour broad-spectrum clock buys us *C. diff* and resistance for a benefit that isn't demonstrated outside shock. I'll dissent formally if it's universal.

[13:14] Tran: And I'll say on the record that stratifying gives people an excuse to be slow on someone who's about to crash but doesn't "officially" meet shock yet.

[13:16] Rao: That's the real risk, and it's a recognition problem, not a timing-target problem. If the screen is mandatory and fires on hypotension or lactate over four, you've identified the shock patients fast. The one-hour clock attaches to *that* trigger.

[13:19] Soto: I can live with that. One hour when the shock criteria fire — hypotension needing pressors, or lactate ≥ 4. Three hours for sepsis without those, with selection off the stewardship empiric table.

[13:21] Tran: And the screen is genuinely mandatory? Not "encouraged"?

[13:22] Mwangi: Mandatory. Discrete EHR field, every triage and every shift assessment on 4West and the ICU. If it's optional we're back where we started.

[13:24] Tran: Then I can sign stratified — one hour for shock, three for non-shock — *if* recognition is mandatory and the shock trigger is unambiguous. That addresses my "excuse to be slow" worry.

[13:26] Holloway: That's the decision. Stratified timing, mandatory screening, plus the repeat-lactate at three hours Priyanka asked for. Elena, no dissent?

[13:27] Soto: No dissent. This is the evidence-based design. Thank you, Marcus, for moving.

[13:28] Tran: Recognition was the missing piece. Fine.

[13:30] Holloway: Last thing — Sandra flagged the compliance number again. We are NOT reporting a single SEP-1 baseline until manual and EHR are reconciled. v2 will say so explicitly. Escalation mechanism is still open; I'm taking the dedicated-nurse proposal to Staffing. We'll close that separately. Good work, all.
