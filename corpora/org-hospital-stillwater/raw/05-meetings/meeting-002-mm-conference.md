# Meeting 002: Morbidity & Mortality Conference — SE-2026-003

**Date:** February 25, 2026
**Location:** Auditorium
**Attendees:** Karen Holloway (facilitator), Marcus Tran (ED), Priyanka Rao (ICU), Elena Soto (ID), Janet Mwangi (Nursing), plus department staff
**Format:** Closed peer-review M&M (non-punitive)

---

[07:03] Holloway: This is peer review, non-punitive, privileged. We're reviewing the January 12 case and the three Q4 look-back cases the preliminary RCA flagged. The final report is being written now; what we say here informs it.

[07:07] Tran: The index case is clear. No triage screen, antibiotics at three hours forty, progressed to shock, died day two. If we'd screened and dosed at one hour, different story. That's the case for moving fast.

[07:10] Rao: I don't disagree on the index case. I want to talk about the three look-back cases, because I don't think they're all the same. One of them — the 78-year-old — had a DNR and a comfort-care order in the chart from before the sepsis episode. That patient was not going to the ICU regardless of bundle timing. Calling that death "preventable" because a lactate was late isn't honest.

[07:13] Holloway: That's an important distinction for the final report. Sandra's adjudicating each case. Priyanka, can you put that in writing for the RCA team?

[07:14] Rao: Yes. My read is two attributable, not three. The comfort-care case shouldn't be in the preventable column.

[07:16] Soto: On the index case — was it actually shock on arrival, or did it become shock? Because that changes what the "right" antibiotic timing even was. If it wasn't shock at presentation, a one-hour mandate wouldn't necessarily have changed the antibiotic, just started the clock earlier. I'm not minimizing it. I'm saying "one hour for everyone" isn't the clean lesson some will want to draw from this.

[07:19] Tran: Or it's exactly the lesson. We waited because we were unsure, and being unsure killed time.

[07:20] Soto: Being unsure is also when you do harm by reflexively broad-spectrum dosing people who turn out to have heart failure or a PE. We'll have this out at the workshop.

[07:22] Mwangi: The thread through all of these is recognition. Nobody screened. If the screen is mandatory and it fires, the timing argument largely takes care of itself, because the clock starts when it should.

[07:25] Holloway: That's the consensus I'm hearing — recognition first, and the timing target stratified by severity, which is the workshop's job. And the death count for the final report is two attributable plus the index event, pending Sandra's written adjudication. Thank you. This was exactly the discussion we needed.
