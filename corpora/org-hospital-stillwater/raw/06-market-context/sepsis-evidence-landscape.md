# Evidence Landscape — Time-to-Antibiotics in Sepsis

**Date:** February 1, 2026
**Compiled by:** Elena Soto, MD (ID/Stewardship) for the Sepsis Steering Committee
**Document type:** Internal evidence briefing (reference)

---

## Purpose

Summarize, neutrally, what the literature does and does not support about antibiotic timing in sepsis, so the committee designs the protocol from evidence rather than slogans.

## What is well supported

- In **septic shock**, each hour of delay to effective antibiotics is associated with increased mortality in multiple observational cohorts. Urgency here is justified.
- Early **source control** and appropriate empiric selection matter as much as raw speed.

## What is contested

- The **"1-hour bundle"** (Surviving Sepsis Campaign, 2018/2021) was widely criticized and is not universally endorsed. The strong recommendation was downgraded in places precisely because the evidence base for a 1-hour clock in *all* sepsis is weak.
- For **sepsis without shock**, there is **no high-quality evidence** that a 1-hour antibiotic target reduces mortality relative to a 3-hour target. The marginal patients in a 1-hour mandate are disproportionately those who are *not* infected.

## Documented downsides of a universal time-to-antibiotic metric

- Increased broad-spectrum exposure and *C. difficile* risk.
- Pressure to administer antibiotics before blood cultures or adequate assessment.
- Diagnostic anchoring on infection.

## Implication for protocol design

The evidence supports *stratification*: urgency proportional to severity (1-hour for shock; a measured 3-hour pathway for non-shock sepsis with stewardship-guided selection). It does not support a single universal 1-hour broad-spectrum mandate.
