# Reference — CMS SEP-1 (Severe Sepsis & Septic Shock) Bundle

**Date:** October 1, 2025
**Document type:** Standing reference summary (Quality Department)
**Status:** Current external requirement — stable reference, not a Stillwater work product

---

## What SEP-1 is

SEP-1 is the U.S. Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) inpatient quality measure for severe sepsis and septic shock. It is an **all-or-none** measure: a case is compliant only if *every* required element is completed within the specified window. This reference summarizes the elements; it is the durable external standard the hospital is held to and does not change with internal protocol revisions.

## 3-hour bundle (from time of presentation / "time zero")

1. Measure initial serum lactate.
2. Obtain blood cultures **before** administering antibiotics.
3. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics.
4. Begin 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥ 4 mmol/L.

## 6-hour bundle

5. Repeat lactate if the initial lactate was elevated.
6. Apply vasopressors for hypotension that does not respond to fluids (target MAP ≥ 65 mmHg).
7. Reassess volume status and tissue perfusion for persistent hypotension or initial lactate ≥ 4 mmol/L.

## Why "all-or-none" matters for measurement

Because the measure fails a case if any single element is missed or late, a strict CMS abstraction will almost always report a **lower** compliance rate than a system that counts partial or initiated bundles. Any internal dashboard that counts "antibiotic ordered" is measuring something different from SEP-1 and is not directly comparable. This standard is what an external reviewer (e.g., CMS, or an accreditor reviewing sepsis care) will apply.
