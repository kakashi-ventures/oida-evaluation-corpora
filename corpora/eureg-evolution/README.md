## Scenario (self-contained, synthetic)

"Northwind Data Systems", a fictional EU SaaS/AI provider, keeps a regulatory-affairs
knowledge base tracking how its obligations evolve across the **AI Act**, **NIS2**, and the
**GDPR**: binding instruments and official guidance, supervisory-authority opinions,
internal compliance standards, outside-counsel/blog/news analysis, draft & withdrawn
proposals, and working comms (email/slack/memo).

Framework names and Article numbers are **real** (so a domain expert can judge realism);
instrument serials, internal standard codes (e.g. `CS-NIS2-07`, `DT-03`, `DP-02`), dates,
and the company are **synthetic**. The supersession / contradiction / authority
relationships are therefore **grounded in the corpus text itself** (explicit in-text cues),
not in the reader's recall of live EU law.

## Why it is challenging (and what each requirement exercises)

- **R1 temporal currency** — four evolving obligations where the **current** version is the
  **freshest-dated** and carries an explicit in-text supersession cue (GPAI guidance
  interim→final; NIS2 reporting v1→v2; GDPR transfer policy v1→v2; CV-tool limited→high-risk).
- **R1.4 calibrated mix (50/25/25)** — 4 freshness (recency *should* win) · 2 **stable**
  (timeless GDPR Art 6 lawful bases; NIS2 essential/important definitions — a fresher
  blog/news must **not** demote them) · 2 **distractor** (the in-force Art 5 prohibited list
  and the DPIA policy are **older** than a fresh **false-cue** competitor).
- **R3 query-conditioned authority** — `q12` (authoritative **is** the answer: GDPR Art 83
  fine tier vs a high-cosine fines blog) and `q13` (authoritative is **not** the answer: the
  NIS2 standard states the duty, but the actual 24h CSIRT endpoint lives in an engineer's email).
- **R4 cross-doc contradiction** — `q09` two supervisory authorities disagree on whether
  pseudonymised telemetry is personal data; `q10` official guidance vs an outside-counsel
  alert on internal-only models; `q11` two internal memos on a system's high-risk status.
- **R5 traps incl. FALSE supersession cues** — a **withdrawn** Art 5 amendment ("would
  replace the current list"), a **not-adopted** 12h NIS2 draft, a fresh email falsely
  "updating" the DPIA policy, and a blog falsely claiming the AI Act "repeals" GDPR Art 22.

## Epistemic-class design (Part-2 R6 readiness — the anchor lesson)

OIDA decay is **per-class** (`oida-core .../epistemic-classes.ts`), and decay is what
makes recency a ranking lever — so each cluster's intended class carries its temporal test:

- **Freshness (C1–C4)** — current + superseded in the **same decaying** class
  (guidance / classifications → `INTERPRETATION` 198d; internal compliance positions →
  `EVALUATION` 198d) so **age separates them and recency can win**. (This is why C2/C3 are
  framed as dated *compliance positions*, not timeless "standards/policies" — a `POLICY`
  tag would be no-decay and recency could never produce a margin.)
- **Stable (C5–C6)** — **no-decay** `CONSTRAINT` (timeless statute/definition).
- **Distractor (C7–C8)** — no-decay current vs a fresher **false-cue** in a decaying class
  (`CLAIM`/`SIGNAL`): the engine must resist the fresh cue, not ride recency.

`class_intent` in the builder is the **intended** class only — OIDA infers the real class
from text at ingest. **Part-2 must verify** the freshness clusters actually land in a
decaying class before the R6 recency margin is trusted; the anchor pilot's measured R6 is
the authority. If one classifies no-decay, re-frame that doc (the anchor's exact lesson).

## Seed → final dimensions (D3 *pilot-then-repower*)

This is a **seed** at the anchor's scale (13 queries). The per-stratum counts (4 freshness /
2 stable / 2 distractor; R3 = 1:1; R4 = 3) are **below the D3 floor of ≥40–50/stratum** by
design — the floors are met by **repowering after the anchor pilot** estimates the
effect-size/variance and Δ. **R5 already clears its ≥80 floor (86)** via dense, genuine
trap cross-listing. T/M/K stay unlocked (spec §3) until the team sets them with the pilot.
