# Meeting 003: Marrowbank Community & Stakeholder Consultation Event

**Date:** October 28, 2025
**Location:** Calder Town Hall
**Present:** James Crowe (chairing, Galewright), Eleanor Vance, Sophie Larsson; Marrow Bird Trust; Calder Coast Fishermen's Association (CCFA); a regional construction union representative; members of the public
**Purpose:** Statutory consultation event — present the project, hear responses

---

[18:30] Crowe: Thank you all for coming. This is part of our formal consultation. We'll present the project briefly and then this is your time — we're here to listen, including to disagreement.

[18:40] Crowe: On benefits — and I want to be straight with this room, because there's been a number circulating. Our early community material put out a figure of 1,200 jobs. I have to be honest that that figure was a developer-wide, best-case number and we cannot substantiate it as local jobs for Marrowbank. We are reviewing it and will correct it. What we can stand behind today is on the order of 300 construction roles at peak; the longer-term operations jobs depend on decisions not yet made. I'd rather tell you that than repeat a number I can't defend.

[18:46] Marrow Bird Trust: We appreciate the candour on jobs, but our objection is about the kittiwakes. Your draft says 120 collisions a year and "low significance." On a realistic avoidance rate we get about 600 a year, and high significance, on colonies already in trouble. We've put that in writing. We do not accept the project's bird assessment.

[18:51] Larsson: I won't pretend that gap away. It comes down to the avoidance rate in the model. We've used the rate in current guidance; you're arguing for a more precautionary one. That's a real and legitimate scientific dispute and it'll be tested at examination. We'll engage on the parameters.

[18:55] CCFA: And the fishermen. The array sits on our crab and lobster grounds, and the cable to Drover's Gap crosses our static gear. We've fished the Marrowbank for generations. We've had no serious conversation about co-existence or compensation. A jobs number for the town doesn't pay for the grounds you're taking from us.

[19:01] Crowe: That's a fair challenge and I accept the fisheries engagement hasn't been good enough. We will set up a proper fisheries liaison and talk co-existence and a construction disruption arrangement. I'll follow up directly.

[19:06] Union representative: For balance — the construction trades want this built. Even a few hundred skilled roles and the supply-chain work matter on this coast. We'd ask that whatever the final number is, it's a real commitment to local hiring, not just a headline.

[19:12] Vance: Heard. We'll come back on each of these — the bird modelling, fisheries co-existence, and a corrected, honest benefits statement. Thank you for holding us to it.

[19:18] Crowe: Closing — the written consultation remains open; please put responses in formally so they're on the record for the examination.
