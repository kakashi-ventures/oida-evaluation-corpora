## 1. Purpose

A short orientation on the revenue and competitive landscape for a GB offshore wind project of Marrowbank's size, to frame the finance case. This is background; the project's own numbers (yield, cost, strike) are developed in the resource assessment and the financial model.

## 2. Route to market — the CfD

The principal route to market for new GB offshore wind is the **Contract for Difference (CfD)**: a long-term, government-backed contract that pays the generator the difference between a fixed strike price and the reference market price, awarded through competitive allocation rounds. A CfD substantially de-risks revenue and is, in practice, the basis on which large offshore projects are financed. Marrowbank's base plan is to contract the bulk of output under a CfD, with the balance exposed to the merchant market.

## 3. LCOE and competition

Allocation rounds are competitive on price, so the **levelised cost of energy (LCOE)** is decisive: projects that can credibly offer a lower LCOE win contracts. The main LCOE levers for a project like ours are turbine size (larger machines lower cost per MW), capacity factor (site quality and availability), capital cost, and cost of capital. This is the commercial logic behind selecting the largest credible turbine and securing a high-quality grid connection.

## 4. Cost environment

The briefing period has seen real cost pressure in offshore wind supply chains — larger turbines and foundations, vessel constraints, and commodity and financing costs — which has pushed project capital costs up relative to the lows of a few years ago. Project cost estimates should be expected to rise through development as scope firms up, not fall.

## 5. Implication for Marrowbank

The case rests on a competitive LCOE: a large turbine, a good site, a well-optimised connection, and a disciplined capital cost, contracted via a CfD. The finance model develops the specific numbers; this briefing is the landscape they sit in.
